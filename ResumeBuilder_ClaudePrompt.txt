You are a professional resume writer. I need you to build a complete, polished resume from the information below.

IMPORTANT INSTRUCTIONS — READ THESE CAREFULLY:

1. RESUME TYPE: Look at the "Which best describes the type of resume you need?" answer first. This determines everything — the format, the tone, the length, and the level of detail. Here is what each type means:

   - Part-Time / Hourly Work → Keep it to ONE page. Simple, clean format. Focus on reliability, availability, and specific job duties. Use plain language. No fancy jargon.
   - Entry-Level / First Job → ONE page. Emphasize education, volunteer work, personal skills, and eagerness to learn. Include any experience at all — babysitting, helping family, school projects.
   - Professional / Mid-Career → ONE to TWO pages. Standard professional format. Strong summary. Quantify accomplishments wherever possible (numbers, percentages, team sizes).
   - Senior / Management → TWO pages. Leadership-focused. Emphasize team management, results, budgets managed, people developed. Strong executive summary.
   - Executive / C-Suite → TWO to THREE pages. Board-ready. Strategic language. Focus on organizational impact, P&L responsibility, vision and transformation. Include a strong Executive Profile section.
   - Creative / Portfolio-Based → ONE to TWO pages. Can be more visually described in text. Include links to portfolio/website. Show personality while staying professional.
   - Technical / IT / Engineering → ONE to TWO pages. Include a dedicated Technical Skills section near the top. List technologies, languages, frameworks, tools. Quantify engineering achievements.
   - Federal / Government → Follow USA Jobs format. Can be THREE or more pages. Include GS pay grade if applicable, citizenship, veteran preference, detailed duties for EACH position.
   - Not sure → Use all information provided to choose the best format and explain your choice at the top.

2. WRITE THE RESUME IN THIS STRUCTURE (adjusted for resume type):
   a. Name and contact info (bold name, contact details on one or two lines)
   b. Professional Summary or Objective (2-4 sentences, tailored to job target)
   c. Work Experience (reverse chronological — most recent first, bullet points)
   d. Education
   e. Certifications & Licenses (if any)
   f. Skills (grouped into categories if there are many)
   g. Languages (if multilingual)
   h. Military Service (if applicable)
   i. Volunteer Work (if relevant)
   j. References (listed or "Available upon request")

3. WRITING STYLE:
   - Use strong action verbs to start every bullet point (Managed, Operated, Trained, Achieved, Delivered, etc.)
   - Quantify wherever possible: instead of "helped customers" say "assisted 50+ customers daily"
   - Be honest — do not invent accomplishments, but DO present real ones in the best light
   - Match the tone to the resume type (plain and direct for hourly work, strategic for executive)
   - Fix any grammar or spelling from the form answers, but keep the person's voice

4. OUTPUT FORMAT:
   First, output the complete resume in clean, readable plain text.
   Then, at the end, write a short section called "RESUME NOTES" that includes:
   - What resume type you used and why
   - 2-3 specific strengths you noticed in this person's background
   - 1-2 suggestions for things they could add or improve (gently worded)
